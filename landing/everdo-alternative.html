An open-source Everdo alternative

Everdo got the important part right: a real GTD app that keeps your data on your own machine and doesn’t make you sign up. If that philosophy is what you want, and the $99.99 Pro license, the five-project free tier, or the closed source are what you don’t, Mindwtr covers the same ground. It is free software under AGPL-3.0 with no paid tier, it works offline by default, it never asks for an account, and it syncs over whatever route you already trust. Here is the honest comparison.

What changes when you switch

The daily workflow barely moves. You still get an Inbox you process, Next and Waiting and Someday lists, Areas of responsibility, sequential projects that only surface their first action, contexts and tags, and a guided Weekly Review. What changes is the fine print. There is no project or area cap to run into, so nothing forces a decision at project number six. There is no license to buy, because Mindwtr is published under AGPL-3.0 — you can read the code that handles your data, build it yourself, and keep using it whether or not the project continues. And multi-device sync doesn’t come with its own bill: pick iCloud, Dropbox, WebDAV, a folder watched by Syncthing, or a small server you host, and every device merges changes locally with a documented conflict-resolution algorithm. File-based targets can carry an optional passphrase, so the files your provider stores are encrypted before they leave the device.

What Everdo does better

Everdo has been shipping since 2018 and its development log is still moving — Desktop 1.11.15 on April 22, 2026 and Android 1.10.0 on May 24, 2026 at the time of writing. That is a longer track record than Mindwtr has, and a mature app shows it in the corners: inline commands, configurable keyboard shortcuts, and years of accumulated fixes. Everdo’s Encrypted Sync Service is easier than what Mindwtr asks of you: you sign up, it works over the internet, and end-to-end encryption is on by default, where Mindwtr expects you to choose and configure a sync provider yourself. Everdo also ships a “Send to Everdo” browser extension for Chrome and Firefox; Mindwtr has no browser extension, so web capture goes through the app or its local API. If a paid, supported service with a person behind it is what you want, the $99.99 license is a reasonable thing to buy.

Moving your data across

There is no one-click Everdo importer. Everdo stores data in its own format, and Mindwtr’s dedicated importers cover Todoist, TickTick, OmniFocus, and DGT GTD. The path from Everdo is the documented generic CSV route, and it takes a spreadsheet detour. Export the whole database as CSV from the Everdo desktop app (CSV export is desktop-only), open it in a spreadsheet editor, and rename the header cells to Mindwtr’s column names — Title, Description, Status, Project, Area, Contexts, Tags, Start Date, Due Date, Completed At. Only Title is required; every other column is optional, so you bring across the ones you use. Then open Settings → Data → Import from Mindwtr CSV, pick the file, and read the preview — it counts the areas, projects, sections, tasks, and checklist items it will create, and lists every warning before anything is written. Mindwtr also saves a recovery snapshot first, so a bad mapping is undoable. Try five rows before you bring over hundreds. The full column reference, including recurrence rules and date formats, is in the Mindwtr CSV import guide.

Common questions

Is Mindwtr really free, or is this a trial?

It is free, and it stays free. There is no trial period, no premium tier, no project cap, no feature paywall, and no in-app purchase. Mindwtr is free software under the AGPL-3.0 license and development is funded by donations.

Where does my data live?

On your device, in a local SQLite database, with JSON and CSV export built in. Nothing is uploaded anywhere unless you configure a sync target yourself. See data & sync for the on-disk format and backup guidance.

Does sync work across devices without paying?

Yes, and over the internet, not just on your home network. Sync is opt-in and runs over a route you choose — iCloud, Dropbox, WebDAV, a Syncthing folder, or a small self-hosted server. Mindwtr operates no sync service of its own, so there is nothing to subscribe to. The trade-off is that you set up the storage yourself.

Do I need an account?

No — not to install, not to sync, not ever. Like Everdo’s offline mode, Mindwtr has no sign-up step. Unlike Everdo’s Encrypted Sync Service, multi-device sync doesn’t introduce one either.

Can I leave again later?

Any time. Your data is already on your disk in a documented format, and Settings → Data → Backup exports CSV or JSON. No export fee, no account to close, nothing to cancel — and because the source is public, you can keep running the version you have.
